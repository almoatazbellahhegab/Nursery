Product objective
Give childcare centers one system to manage children, parents, staff, attendance, daily activities, communication, billing, pickup authorization, and parent engagement.
This pack converts the supplied vision into product work the team can refine, estimate, implement, and test. It intentionally removes prescribed technologies, vendor products, database structures, and code-level instructions. The Technical Lead remains accountable for architecture and implementation decisions that satisfy the outcomes, controls, and acceptance criteria defined here.

How to Use This Pack
The backlog is an outcome and behavior baseline, not a substitute for discovery, UX design, security review, legal review, or engineering design. Product and QA should use the acceptance criteria as the starting test oracle. Engineering and the Technical Lead should validate feasibility and split items further when an independently valuable, testable increment can be released.
Complexity is preliminary product judgment only. The Technical Lead and Development Team must validate estimates after solution design and refinement.
Foundation items are prerequisites or cross-cutting controls. MVP Phase 1 establishes safe daily operations. Phase 2 adds revenue, communication, media, pickup, and growth workflows. Phase 3 and Future Modules require separate approval.
All role, privacy, financial, safety, and cross-organization denial criteria are release blockers for the affected feature.
Where the source specified a technology, the backlog retains the intended product outcome and leaves the method to the Technical Lead.
Confirmed Product Direction
Initial customers are nurseries, daycare centers, preschools, and early-childhood centers.
The product is one configurable, multi-organization SaaS platform that supports multiple branches and future white-label offerings.
The parent experience should encourage daily use; staff workflows must be fast enough for repeated classroom use.
Arabic and English are initial languages, with Arabic right-to-left support and region-aware dates, time, currency, and notifications.
Children’s medical, identity, relationship, pickup, incident, and media information is highly sensitive.
Full payroll, real-time vehicle tracking, commerce, dedicated branded store builds, and parent community are not required for the initial MVP.
Assumptions Requiring Validation
The initial market is Egypt, but no legal, privacy, tax, payment, identity-verification, or record-retention policy has yet been confirmed.
One person may hold different roles or relationships across organizations; conflict and precedence rules require approval.
The center, not the platform operator, is expected to control most child records and consents; legal ownership and processor responsibilities require specialist review.
MVP Phase 1 includes attendance, daily timeline, parent home, announcements, notifications, basic administration, and the minimum foundations needed to operate them safely.
Online payments are Phase 2; the payment provider and supported markets are Technical Lead and commercial decisions, subject to finance and legal review.
Plan names and example entitlements are commercial hypotheses, not approved pricing or packaging.
Product Non Goals for Initial MVP
Full payroll, leave management, and advanced shift management.
Real-time vehicle location tracking.
Parent social community and parent-to-parent messaging.
Commerce catalog, fulfillment, platform commissions, and marketplace operations.
Dedicated app-store builds, custom domains, and public integration APIs.
Medical diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or clinical decision support.

Common Definition of Done
The following applies to every feature in addition to its feature-specific Definition of Done:
All acceptance criteria pass, including permitted and denied paths.
Functional, integration, regression, role-permission, and risk-based negative tests pass at the appropriate test level.
Organization, branch, classroom, relationship, and role boundaries are verified where relevant.
Arabic and English content, right-to-left behavior, responsive layouts, accessibility, and large touch targets are validated for affected journeys.
Sensitive actions are auditable, sensitive data is minimized, and privacy or consent behavior is verified.
Performance, pagination, temporary-connectivity behavior, retry behavior, and duplicate prevention are validated where relevant.
Product, Design, QA, and business representatives complete required validation; specialist security, privacy, medical, finance, or legal review is completed where applicable.
User-facing help, operational guidance, support notes, release notes, known limitations, and relevant product documentation are updated.

Cross Cutting Quality Requirements
Security and isolation: No user may access another organization’s records. Access must also respect branch, classroom, assignment, guardian relationship, role, consent, and task need. Hiding a control is not sufficient; unauthorized requests must fail.
Privacy and child safety: Collect only necessary data, default to private, protect medical and media information, prevent public exposure, and require explicit consent for marketing use. Specialist privacy and child-safeguarding review is required.
Reliability and retries: Safety, finance, notification, invitation, audit, media, and batch processes must tolerate retry without duplicate or conflicting outcomes. Failed actions must be visible and recoverable.
Performance and scale: List views must avoid loading unbounded records, support pagination or progressive loading, and remain usable at realistic organization and platform volumes defined by the Technical Lead and Product.
Temporary connectivity: Teachers must be able to record approved basic activities during short connectivity interruptions. Synchronization must avoid loss and duplicates and must surface conflicts.
Usability: Staff workflows use large touch targets, clear status, minimal forms, and fast repeated entry. Parent views present important information within seconds and avoid an enterprise resource planning feel.
Localization: All released visible content supports Arabic and English. Right-to-left layout, dates, currency, times, dynamic notification text, and mixed-direction content must be tested.
Accessibility: Color is not the sole status indicator; text and controls remain readable and operable across supported devices, zoom, and assistive settings defined during design.
Observability and support: Operational failures, delivery failures, and data-quality issues must be discoverable by authorized support roles without exposing unnecessary child data.
Data lifecycle: Retention, export, deletion, suspension, archival, and module-disable behavior must be explicit and testable; historical and audit integrity must be preserved where policy requires.

Risks and Product Recommendations
Safety and privacy policy gap: Pickup verification, medication, incidents, medical visibility, consent, retention, and support access require approved policies before release. Recommendation: Product to convene legal, privacy, safeguarding, and operations review before detailed build of affected modules.
MVP breadth: The source includes more than a viable first release and mixes core operations with revenue and future expansion. Recommendation: Protect Phase 1 exit criteria and do not pull Phase 3 modules forward unless they resolve a Phase 1 blocker.
Permission complexity: Role names alone cannot express classroom, child, relationship, branch, consent, and task-based access. Recommendation: Approve a detailed permission matrix and denial test suite before development of dependent features.
Offline conflicts: Batch care and attendance entry during connectivity loss can create duplicate or contradictory records. Recommendation: Define user-visible conflict rules and immutable event identity during Technical Lead design.
Payment and financial regulation: Provider choice, tax, refunds, chargebacks, receipts, and local payment rules are unresolved. Recommendation: Complete market, finance, legal, and reconciliation discovery before FEAT-028 enters implementation.
Notification fatigue: High-frequency care events can reduce trust if each triggers an alert. Recommendation: Validate mandatory categories and batching rules with parents and centers before final notification design.
Commercial assumptions: Starter, Growth, and Pro packaging is illustrative and lacks pricing research. Recommendation: Treat entitlements as configurable and validate packaging separately from engineering delivery.
Analytics ambiguity: Metrics such as churn, occupancy, engagement, and collection rate can be misleading without definitions. Recommendation: Create a metric dictionary with owner, formula, exclusions, refresh timing, and source before dashboard acceptance.
